between frequency bands).

#### 3. The Coupling Term Saga (~3 pages)

This is the heart of the paper. Three approaches, each with a
clear failure mode:

**3a. D-field constitutive approach.**
Folding χ⁽²⁾ into the D→E relation: E₂ = (D₂ - ε₀χ⁽²⁾dE₁²)/(ε₀n₂²).
Produces a static perturbation that doesn't accumulate. Same ~5%
conversion regardless of crystal length or pulse duration.

Diagnosis: the constitutive approach gives the instantaneous local
perturbation but doesn't drive ∂P_NL/∂t into the wave equation.

**3b. ΔP_NL source term (∂P_NL/∂t).**
ΔE₂ = -χ⁽²⁾d(E₁_new² - E₁_old²)/n₂². Correctly accumulates SH
(verified: E₂ grows through crystal, detuning kills it). But ppw²
convergence: 4% at ppw=20, 58% at ppw=80, 78% at ppw=100.

**3c. Wave equation source (∂²P_NL/∂t²).**
Attempted: ΔE₂ = -χ⁽²⁾d(E₁²_{n+1} - 2E₁²_n + E₁²_{n-1})/n₂².
100× weaker than ΔP_NL, sign-independent. Diagnosis: second-order
source incompatible with first-order Yee E-update. The Δt²
"cancellation" from the wave equation only applies to the
second-order wave equation update, not to the first-order Yee
leapfrog.

**Figure 4: Coupling convergence.** Plot E₂/E₁ vs ppw for all three
approaches. Log-log or semi-log showing the ppw² scaling of the
ΔP_NL approach, the flat (non-convergent) constitutive approach,
and the ppw-dependent-but-100×-weaker wave equation approach.
Include the CW theoretical prediction as a horizontal line.

**Figure 5: Spatial SH profile.** Frozen snapshot of |E₂(z)|
(Hilbert envelope) under the pump pulse, showing the asymmetric
ramp characteristic of coherent accumulation. Compare matched vs
detuned Λ.

Lesson: in carrier-resolved FDTD, the coupling formulation must
match the time-integration order of the host scheme. First-order
Yee needs a first-order (∂P_NL/∂t) source; second-order sources
are incompatible regardless of their formal accuracy.

#### 4. Numerical Dispersion Mismatch (~3 pages)

The most important finding. The ppw² scaling was initially
attributed to the coupling finite-difference accuracy. It's
actually caused by numerical dispersion mismatch between the
two grids.

**4a. The mechanism.** Each Yee grid has a slightly wrong phase
velocity at its carrier frequency. The errors differ between the
two grids (different ω, different n). The difference creates a
parasitic Δk that the physical poling period doesn't compensate.

Derive the parasitic coherence length:
```
δ(Δk) = [k₂_num - k₂] - 2[k₁_num - k₁]
L_coh_parasitic = π / |δ(Δk)|
```

Show that L_coh_parasitic ∝ ppw², directly explaining the observed
convergence rate.

**Figure 6: Parasitic coherence length vs ppw.** Plot L_coh_parasitic
against ppw, with the crystal length as a horizontal reference line.
Where L_coh < L_crystal, the SH oscillates and net conversion is
small. Where L_coh > L_crystal, conversion approaches the CW limit.
Mark the observed conversion efficiencies at each ppw.

**4b. The fix.** Adjust permittivity on each grid so numerical phase
velocity matches physical:
```
n_eff = S · sin(k_true·Δz/2) / sin(ω·Δt/2)
```

One-time initialization, zero runtime cost.

**Figure 7: ppw convergence before/after correction.** Same format
as Figure 4 but showing the ΔP_NL approach with and without the
dispersion correction. The corrected curve should be much flatter.

**4c. Generality.** This effect arises whenever two carrier-resolved
FDTD grids are coupled for any nonlinear process (SFG, DFG, OPA,
THG). It's not specific to PPLN or QPM. The correction is general
and should be applied whenever multiple Yee grids are coupled at
different carrier frequencies.

Lesson: in coupled-grid FDTD, the dominant source of error is not
the coupling term — it's the linear propagator. Sub-percent
numerical dispersion errors, invisible on a single grid, create
cumulative phase mismatch between grids that destroys the nonlinear
coupling coherence.

#### 5. Energy Conservation and Manley-Rowe (~2 pages)

**5a. What's conserved.** EM energy (ε₁E₁² + ε₂E₂²) is NOT
conserved during SHG — it should increase. The conserved quantity
is Manley-Rowe (photon number): ε₁E₁²/ω₁ + ε₂E₂²/ω₂.

**5b. Coupling constant ratio.** The MR requirement constrains the
ratio of the SHG and back-conversion coupling constants. The naive
derivation gave nl₁/nl₂ = 2n₂²/n₁² but MR requires n₂²/(2n₁²) —
a factor of 4.

**5c. Per-cell projection failure.** The per-cell MR projection
(rescaling E₁ per cell) diverges at carrier zero crossings, creating
Nyquist checkerboard instability. Document the failure mode with
figures.

**Figure 8: Checkerboard instability.** Plot of E₂(z) showing the
onset and growth of alternating-sign spatial oscillations at the
grid scale. Include the timeline from the diagnostic data.

**5d. Global projection.** Scalar correction applied uniformly
across the crystal after each coupling step. Stable, exact MR
conservation, minimal perturbation to fields.

**Figure 9: Conservation diagnostics.** Three-panel time series
showing (a) MR/MR₀ staying at 1.0, (b) U_total/U₀ correctly
increasing, (c) ΔU vs ΔU₂/2 confirming the energy comes from
the nonlinear polarization work.

Lesson: conservation in coupled nonlinear FDTD requires explicit
enforcement. The coupling constants must satisfy the MR ratio, and
a global projection cleans up residual drift without introducing
spatial instabilities.

#### 6. Remaining Limitations and the Path Forward (~1 page)

Frank accounting:

- ppw dependence reduced but not eliminated by dispersion correction
  (residual from coupling finite-difference, coupling constant ratio
  approximation at finite Δt)
- Explicit coupling unstable in extreme pump depletion at high ppw
  without global MR projection
- No dispersion within each grid's bandwidth (GVD absent)
- Unidirectional would be more natural for this physics (the
  reflected pulse at crystal faces is physical but not the focus)
- Carrier resolution imposes a minimum grid density that limits
  practical crystal length (~1-5 mm at interactive rates)

Briefly note that envelope methods (split-step Fourier) eliminate
all of these issues simultaneously, at the cost of not resolving
the carrier. Point forward without developing the topic — that's
a separate document.
